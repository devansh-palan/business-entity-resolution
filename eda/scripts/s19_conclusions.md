## Reading the sweep

**The optimum is a precision-heavy threshold, and it is flat.** Macro F0.5 rises steeply from 0.763 at t = 0.01 to 0.947 at 0.21 and 0.966 at 0.50, peaks at 0.970 around 0.70-0.75, and falls to 0.916 at 0.99. Between 0.63 and 0.80 it stays within 0.001 of the peak, so the exact value hardly matters, and the report half's own optimum (0.70, 0.9699) is within 0.0001 of the value chosen on the tuning half (0.75, 0.9698). The competition metric weights precision four times more than recall, which is why the peak sits where mean precision is 0.988 and mean recall 0.940, not at the balanced point (t = 0.35-0.40, where both are about 0.965).

**What each threshold buys, on the report half.** From t = 0.50 to 0.75 the false merges halve (4,032 to 1,926 pairs) and the singleton score climbs from 0.889 to 0.945, while mean recall drops from 0.957 to 0.940 and one-match entities lose 0.005. Going further to 0.90 cuts the false merges to 839 and the singleton score reaches 0.973, but recall falls to 0.911 and one-match entities to 0.894: at that point the missed matches cost more than the avoided merges. The predicted list sizes barely move (mean 3.4 to 3.2 matches per entity, median 3 throughout), because most true matches score above 0.9 and most non-matches below 0.1; the threshold only arbitrates the few percent of pairs in between.

**A single global threshold is appropriate.** Thresholds chosen separately per country (India 0.74, US 0.75), per candidate-count band (0.68-0.75), per source of the pair (0.75 and 0.75), or per band of the entity's best probability (0.50-0.75) change the report-half macro F0.5 by -0.0001 to +0.0003, all within the noise of 55,170 entities. The segments' own optima differ by less than the width of the plateau, so there is nothing for a per-segment rule to exploit. The one segmentation that would pay is the oracle by the true number of matches (0.99 for singletons, 0.68-0.69 for one to five matches, 0.47 for six or more: +0.0038), which says that an entity-level predictor of "how many matches does this entity have" could be worth up to 0.4 points; the best probability of the entity, the nearest observable proxy, captures 0.0003 of it. That is the only route to a non-global rule worth building, and it is a modelling task, not a threshold choice.

**Decision for the pipeline.** One global threshold of 0.75 on the prior-corrected LightGBM probability (0.70 would do equally well), plus pool-side uniqueness, which adds 0.0002. The threshold should be re-checked once the model is retrained on all folds, since the calibration will shift slightly; the plateau makes the check cheap.
